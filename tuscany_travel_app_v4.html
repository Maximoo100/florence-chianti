Florence
Firenze · Days 1–3 · 21–23 June

Florence is the jewel of the Italian Renaissance — a compact, walkable city that contains more great art per square mile than almost anywhere on earth. Built on the banks of the Arno and dominated by Brunelleschi's extraordinary terracotta dome, the city has barely changed in outline since the Medici ruled it in the 15th century.

Its streets are lined with palaces, churches, medieval towers and artisan workshops. The Uffizi holds the greatest collection of Renaissance painting in the world. The Accademia houses Michelangelo's David. And yet Florence never feels like a museum — it is a living, breathing city with fantastic food, superb wine bars and a proud local identity quite distinct from Rome or Venice.

The Oltrarno neighbourhood on the south side of the Arno is quieter and more artisan — full of goldsmiths, leather workers, independent restaurants and locals going about their daily lives. Crossing Ponte Vecchio at dusk with a gelato in hand as the light turns golden on the river is one of travel's great simple pleasures.

Radda in Chianti
Chianti Classico · Days 4–7 · 24–28 June

Radda in Chianti sits at the heart of the Chianti Classico wine region, perched on a hilltop between Florence and Siena at around 530 metres. It is one of the best-preserved medieval villages in Tuscany — encircled by ancient walls, its narrow streets lined with stone houses, wine shops and small trattorias.

The surrounding landscape is the quintessential Tuscan postcard — rolling hills covered in vineyards and olive groves, punctuated by cypress trees and the occasional stone farmhouse. In late June the vines are in full leaf and the countryside is at its most lush and beautiful.

The Chianti Classico wine produced here — marked with the iconic black rooster — is among Italy's finest. Estates and cellars dot the hills in every direction, many centuries old. The pace of life is slow, the food is exceptional and the evenings are long and warm.
